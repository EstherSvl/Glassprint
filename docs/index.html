On a tablet that is a few minutes, and the last stretch is compiling rather than downloading, so the megabytes stop moving before it is finished. The browser keeps it all afterwards and later visits start in seconds.

    6 · Fade into the glass

    Manga screentone: tone from dot size, every dot full-strength ink. Gradates solid areas that dissolve cannot touch. Stay at 1 mm or coarser — finer than that beats against the printer's own halftone.

    7 · Glaze

    Repeating one ink only deepens it. Stacking different inks multiplies different spectra together, which reaches colours a single ink can't — subtractive glazing. Uses the glass colour set above the preview.

    8 · Export

    9 · Calibrate to your printer

    Until this is done, every preview assumes a colour's RGB is exactly what it lets through the glass. It isn't — on a real press that's wrong by tens of levels. Print the chart once, photograph it, and the preview becomes a prediction.

    A white base covers the glass, so colour behaves the way it does on card — more predictable, and the glass tint stops mattering. Off is the glaze case, where the glass is doing half the work. They are different printers as far as this is concerned, so calibrate whichever one you are going to use.
